What Results Should You Expect from Lead Generation Services?

On this page

Setting realistic expectations is one of the most important conversations to have before investing in lead generation services. Too many businesses enter agency relationships with expectations shaped by sales pitches rather than actual program performance data - and end up disappointed by results that were perfectly reasonable given the timeline and investment level.

Here is an honest guide to what good lead generation performance looks like at different stages of a program.

The First Four to Eight Weeks: Setup and Early Data

The first phase of any lead generation program is rarely the highest-performing one, and it should not be evaluated as though it is. This is the period when campaigns are being built, initial targeting is being tested, landing pages are being optimized, and the program is generating its first real-world data.

For paid channels - LinkedIn and Google - this period typically produces initial leads but at a higher cost per lead than the program will achieve once it is optimized. For content and organic channels, this period involves creating assets that will take months to generate meaningful traffic. For outbound prospecting, the first sequences are being launched and initial response data is coming in.

What you should be getting in the first eight weeks is transparency about what is being tested, what the early data shows, and what adjustments are being made. Not impressive volume numbers.

Months Two to Four: Optimization and Stabilisation

By the third and fourth month, paid campaigns have accumulated enough data to be optimized meaningfully. The best-performing ad variations have been identified. Targeting parameters have been refined based on which audiences are generating the highest-quality leads. Landing page conversion rates have been improved through testing. And outbound sequences have been refined based on response data from the first batches.

For a well-structured program in this phase, most businesses in Canada, the US, and the UK should expect: ten to thirty qualified leads per month from paid channels (depending on spend level and market competitiveness), initial organic traffic from content beginning to compound, and outbound prospecting generating qualified meetings on a consistent weekly basis.

Months Six and Beyond: Compounding Returns

This is where the investment in sustained lead generation activity starts to produce returns that exceed the individual parts. Content that was written in month two is now ranking and generating organic traffic. Paid campaigns that were optimized in month three are running efficiently. The outbound program has built up a cadence that produces consistent pipeline.

Businesses with six-plus month programs that have been actively managed and optimized typically see cost per qualified lead declining, organic pipeline growing as a percentage of total leads, and a more predictable monthly pipeline that makes revenue forecasting more reliable.

How to Evaluate Your Program Performance

The metrics that matter: cost per qualified lead by channel, lead-to-opportunity conversion rate, pipeline value generated from program activity, and revenue attributed to marketing through closed deals. These should be tracked monthly and shared transparently.

The metrics that do not matter as primary measures: impressions, reach, click-through rate, number of posts, follower growth. These can be useful diagnostic tools, but they should not be the headline metrics in any credible lead generation program.

Frequently Asked Questions

What ROI should I expect from lead generation services?

ROI depends on your average customer value, your close rate from qualified leads, and your program investment. A business where a customer is worth $30,000 in revenue and close rate from qualified leads is 20 percent can justify a significant monthly investment. LeadStrong models this with prospective clients before engagement starts.

How long before lead generation services produce meaningful results?

Paid channels typically produce leads within four to six weeks of launch. Full program optimization and compounding returns from content and organic take three to six months. Businesses should plan for a six to twelve month horizon to evaluate the full return.

What metrics should my lead generation agency be reporting on?

Qualified leads, cost per qualified lead, meetings booked, and pipeline value. If your agency is leading with impressions and clicks as primary success metrics, ask them to reframe reporting around pipeline-connected outcomes.

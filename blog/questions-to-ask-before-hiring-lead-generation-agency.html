Questions to Ask Before Hiring a Lead Generation Agency

On this page

The gap between what lead generation agencies promise and what they deliver is wide enough that asking the right questions before you commit can save you significant time and money. These questions are designed to surface the information that distinguishes a provider who will deliver genuine pipeline from one who will deliver a monthly report full of activity metrics that do not move your business forward.

How Do You Define a Qualified Lead for My Business?

This question comes first because the answer tells you immediately whether the provider is thinking about your business or their own metrics. A good answer requires them to ask about your ideal customer profile, your sales criteria, and what a conversion means for you before they can answer it. If they give you a generic definition of a qualified lead without understanding your specific business, they are not yet thinking about your program - they are thinking about their standard deliverable.

Which Channels Will You Use, and Why These Specifically?

Not "which channels do you offer" but "which channels are right for this specific business and why." The answer should reference your ICP and your market. A B2B business targeting procurement directors at mid-market manufacturers in Canada should hear a different channel recommendation from one targeting founders of early-stage SaaS companies in London.

If the recommendation is the same regardless of who you are, the agency is selling their capability rather than solving your problem.

What Does Success Look Like at Thirty, Sixty, and Ninety Days?

This forces specificity. A credible answer will acknowledge that the first thirty days involve setup and early data gathering, that sixty days typically shows initial campaign performance, and that ninety days is where meaningful optimization data starts to emerge. It will be honest about which channels take longer to produce results and which produce results more quickly. Promises of significant organic results in thirty days are not credible.

Who Will Actually Be Working on My Account?

The senior strategist who presents in the pitch is often not the person who manages the account day to day. Ask specifically who will be running your campaigns, what their experience level is, how many other accounts they manage simultaneously, and how accessible they will be for questions and updates.

What Does Your Reporting Look Like, and Can I See an Example?

Ask to see an actual client report - redacted if necessary. This tells you more than any description of their process. Does it show performance at the campaign and channel level? Does it connect to qualified leads and pipeline? Does it include commentary on what worked and what did not? Or is it a dashboard of impressions and clicks with no narrative?

What Happens If We Are Not Happy with the Results?

How a provider answers this question says a lot about their confidence and their accountability model. The best providers have clear processes for diagnosing underperformance, communicating it honestly, and adjusting the program. They do not promise guaranteed outcomes before understanding your business, but they take responsibility for the quality of their work when it falls short.

Avoid providers that respond to this question by immediately shifting to contract terms and exit clauses. That is a sign they expect disputes.

Frequently Asked Questions

What questions should I ask a lead generation agency before hiring them?

The most important questions are: how they define a qualified lead for your business, which channels they recommend and why, what success looks like at 30/60/90 days, who actually works on your account, what their reporting looks like, and what happens if results fall short.

How do I know if a lead generation agency is trustworthy?

Look for providers who ask more questions than they answer in early conversations, who measure success against pipeline metrics rather than activity metrics, who are transparent about timelines, and who have clear processes for handling underperformance rather than deflecting the question.

What is a reasonable contract length for a lead generation agency?

Initial engagements of three to six months are reasonable, allowing enough time for campaigns to build momentum and be properly assessed. Be cautious of twelve-month lock-ins with no performance provisions.
